# Changelog

## 0.10.2-wip — a new Standard; two Classic defects that put results millimetres off; Instant Meshes installs itself; the first real model logged; SOLID evidence (defensive publication)
- **Standard picks the engine by the part's shape** (new engine `auto`, *Advanced → Surface fitter → Auto*): smooth closed organic parts go to Quad patches (kept when within twice the tolerance), everything else, and organic parts Quad patches can't do well, to the Ladder engine (Quick). Why: the old Standard (Classic) was the same or worse than Quick on nearly every test part, and took 4 min 23 s on a real 91,676-triangle cat where Quick took 81 s with a closer worst point (0.228 against 1.110 mm). Measured on the 12 test meshes: Classic 305 s and 15,744 faces, Quick 140 s and 1,922, the new Standard 176 s and 2,394; on 5 organic shapes the new Standard is the only one with all 5 within the tight tolerance and no facets (219 s against Quick's 96 s). Notes: `docs/research/STANDARD.md`. **Fine** now starts from Standard's engine too (`STL2STEP_BEST_FIRST=classic` for the old first pass). Classic stays in *Advanced*, and the command line and the CAD plug-ins still default to it.
- **Classic and Ladder: results no longer millimetres off the mesh.** (1) A face could be the right size and lie on the mesh but in the wrong place (a cylinder round the other side of an oval wall); the finished solid is now checked the other way round too, and a region whose own triangles aren't covered is rebuilt as facets. The oval plate 3.658 → 0.083 mm. (2) Round faces that OpenCascade can't triangulate after the STEP round trip (they measured as holes, and OpenCascade-based programs would most likely struggle to draw them too) are now found by meshing the whole solid as finely as the deviation is measured, and rebuilt as facets. The blobs plate 3.000 → 0.176 mm. The write-and-read-back check Staged had now runs for Classic and Ladder too. Both cost faces where they act (the oval 141 → 1,223, the blobs plate 824 → 2,384); every other test mesh is unchanged. Switches: `cover_check`, `trip_check`.
- Fixed on the way: meshing a shape a second time could make OpenCascade's mesher run without end (the vase timed out at 30 min with the new checks); the checks mesh a copy.
- `tools/bench.py` takes engine options (`--engines ladder:ladder_guess=false`) and records each run's tolerance.
- **Quad patches on a Mac or Windows without typing anything:** the launchers now install **Instant Meshes** (`pynanoinstantmeshes` 0.0.3, BSD, a ready-built ~1 MB wheel for Apple-silicon macOS, 64-bit Windows and 64-bit Linux) once, as an optional extra listed in `app/requirements-optional.txt`. Ready-built only (`--only-binary`), tried once per list, and a failure never stops the app from starting. Before this the Quad patches engine had no quad remesher on macOS (QuadriFlow ships for Linux only) and handed every part to Classic. Not run on a real Mac or Windows machine here; the requirement line was checked against PyPI's wheels.
- **The first real-world results** (`docs/research/GHOST_CAT.md`): a 91,676-triangle ghost-sheet cat (a third-party Printables model, used as a test only, not in the repository) on an M5 MacBook. Standard 4 min 23 s, 17,197 faces of which 829 NURBS, worst point 1.110 mm (outside its tolerance: an open defect); Quick 81 s, 11,864 faces, 0.228 mm; Draft 79 s, 11,117 faces, 0.271 mm. The face totals and the 50 MB file are mostly facets. The diagnostic QR codes were read back from phone photos of the screen, the first real test of that path.
- **SOLID evidence** (`docs/research/SOLID_EVIDENCE.md`, PDF beside it): a defensive publication of every method in the STL → STEP converter, with parameters, variants, the prior art it does not claim, and all the research findings in one place, so the methods stay free for everyone to use.
- Instant Meshes' own progress printout (about 70 lines from its C++ code) no longer reaches the job's output.
- Tests: `tests/test_standard.py` (a mesh with nothing over it is found and the shape itself is left unmeshed; a good solid has no undrawn faces; Standard is Auto and Fine starts from it; a CAD part goes the Quick way, a smooth blob becomes quad patches within tolerance); the Instant Meshes layout is closed, all quads and prints nothing (skipped where it is not installed); 126 in all.

## 0.10.1-wip — Quad patches engine (QuadriFlow) for organic shapes; round faces on private outlines
- **Quad patches** (experimental, opt-in: *Advanced → Surface fitter → Quad patches*, `--engine quad`): a wireframe first, then surfaces between the wires. The mesh is laid out as quads that follow its curvature by **QuadriFlow** (BSD-3), every quad edge becomes a curve on the mesh, every quad a Coons patch whose inside is fitted to the mesh, all patches solved together with a soft no-crease condition along shared edges; the layout is made finer until within the tolerance. Only for closed organic parts (no real flats, under 0.2 % sharp edges, not a turned shape); anything else goes to Classic, with the reason in the log.
- Measured on five synthetic organic shapes: the only engine within tolerance on all of them, no facets, the smoothest seams (the bumpy ring with a hole: 0.028 mm and 3° worst seam against Classic's 0.144 mm, 50 facet areas and 32°); the 162,000-triangle figure in 7 s against Classic's 421 s; more faces where the shape is busy (the blob 492 against 24) and slower there (38 s against 8 s; the bumpy ring 124 s). Turned shapes and the ridged knob are handed over. Notes: `docs/research/QUADPATCHES.md`.
- **QuadriFlow ships** for 64-bit Linux with glibc 2.38+ (`app/bin/linux-x86_64/quadriflow`, built from pinned sources, every licence in `app/bin/README.txt`); `tools/build_quadriflow.py` builds it anywhere with CMake and a C++ compiler (tested here: same output as the shipped build). Instant Meshes (`pip install pynanoinstantmeshes`) is used when QuadriFlow is not there or cannot start.
- **Round faces on private outlines:** a cylinder or sphere face is now built on its own copy of the outline too (fitted and flat faces got that in 0.10.0), unless its whole outline runs along other round faces (the two halves of a sphere need the shared seam). The knob: 963 → 69 faces (Classic and Quick), 950 → 60 (Staged); the other 11 test meshes unchanged; the 12-mesh totals 13,996 → 13,102 faces (Classic), 1,574 → 680 (Quick), 1,478 → 588 (Staged).
- Tests: `tests/test_quadfit.py` (which parts the engine takes, the quad helpers, the fast nearest-point search against the exact one, the hand-over to Classic, a whole blob conversion within tolerance).

## 0.10.0-wip — repeatable results, faces no longer damage their neighbours, Draft on dense meshes, Quick on extruded parts, faster Jetwash; ImageToSketch 0.10.1
Every number below is from the 2-core Linux test machine, quiet before/after runs of the same meshes; the full tables are in `docs/research/IMPROVEMENTS_0_10.md`.
- **Results are repeatable again.** The vase's Classic result differed between runs (28 or 30 facet areas, noted in 0.9.1). Cause: OpenCascade's plate filler chains the loose outline edges it is given through a map hashed on memory addresses, so which edge it started from, and whether it ended up asking a null vertex for its point, changed with where things landed in memory. The filler now gets its outline as one chained wire, as its own copy (it alters the edges it is given), and edges shorter than its own tolerance are left out. Three runs of the vase: identical.
- **A face's repair can no longer damage its neighbours.** OpenCascade's face repair widens outline vertices to make a wire close on a surface that misses them; on a *shared* outline that widening reached the faces next door (a knob vertex went from 0.03 mm to 4.7 mm of tolerance), after which every check let a neighbouring face 1.3 mm off through, and many good patches were turned into facets by the same accident. Fitted (NURBS) and flat faces are now built and repaired on their own copy of the outline, and a face whose repair had to widen the outline by more than twice the tolerance is refused as a fit. Classic on the knob: 1,103 faces and 0.129 mm → 963 faces and 0.058 mm; the torus 0.185 → 0.160 mm. Round faces keep the shared outline (the two halves of a sphere only build because the first half's repair fixes the shared seam for the second).
- **Draft finishes on dense flat-sided meshes.** The flat-face retriangulation that the Draft preset's reduction relies on fanned the same triangle twice where two straight outline stretches met at one corner (16 open edges on the 220,000-triangle tray), and earcut's zero-area triangles from collinear outline points could not become faces, so the reduction was thrown away and the full mesh fitted. Fixed; the reduction now gives a closed 48,400-triangle mesh in 0.8 s. Also fixed: the preset's 60 s refining cap counted from the moment the mesh was replaced by its reduced copy, so on reduced meshes it never fired.
- **Quick (the Ladder engine) on extruded parts:** the prismatic route now claims extruded outlines first (the Staged roughing), which on five of seven such test parts gave far fewer faces and a closer fit at the same speed; when the roughed result is more than twice the tolerance from the mesh, on the prismatic or the lathe route, the plain fitter is run as well and the better result kept. Over the 12 test meshes Quick's faces went from 4,767 to 1,561 and its worst deviation from 3.0 mm to 0.19 mm; it is slower where the fallback runs (the oval 9 → 27 s). `--no-ladder-rough` keeps the old behaviour.
- **Jetwash:** candidates are removed in batches of up to 16 faces while batches succeed, then one at a time (defeaturing costs about 0.3 s a face up to there and far more beyond: the old all-at-once attempt on the tray's 81 faces cost 38 s and failed), and the volume test runs before the slower geometry check. The fine tray 102 → 63 s, every one of the 12 test files identical.
- **Facets join without sewing when every edge is shared** (the fully faceted fallback and checkpoint; sewing alone took 69 s of the 48,400-facet tray's assembly); fitted faces still need sewing, as before.
- The point-on-surface projector is set up once per surface (identical results, a tenth to a third off the plate filler's measuring loop). Unused code removed on the way.
- **ImageToSketch 0.10.1** built in (`pairdiff.py` new; `calsheet.py`, `scaletoken.py`, `pipeline.py`, `service.py`, `__init__.py` changed). With the calibration sheet chosen, an **empty-sheet photo** of the same sheet without the part can be given (button under the picture): lined up through the markers, what did not change and the part's shadow are made plain paper before tracing. Also fixed in the library: spikes the side-wall correction could leave on an outline. Simulated photos only, as before. The page was changed by the module's `tools/add_empty_sheet_photo.py` (one-off, not kept). Test: a drawn plate with a hard shadow on the sheet, with and without the empty-sheet photo (the shadow's borderline outline goes; a missing second photo is reported).
- Also looked at, nothing to change in this app's own code: the SCAD → STEP tab spends its time in OpenSCAD itself (the stress model: 0.3 s of geometry here, 18 s in OpenSCAD's verification render), the Image → STEP tab in ImageToSketch's marker detection (1.8 s a conversion). Two ideas from the chat (slicing with a 0.0001 mm nozzle; a wireframe filled with surfaces for organic shapes) are weighed in the notes.
- Tests: 9 new (retriangulation and a dense reduction, edge chaining, the budget's start time, Jetwash batches, the Ladder's prismatic route and a rounded extrusion, the empty-sheet photo); 114 in all.

## 0.9.1-wip — the heavy lifting checked: two Python bottlenecks out, old releases pruned
- **Native-code check** (`docs/research/NATIVE.md`): every dependency listed by what it runs as. OpenCascade, numpy, scipy, GEOS, libspatialindex and OpenCV already did the heavy lifting; two places where Python sat in the way were fixed:
  - **Winding repair without networkx:** trimesh fixed flipped triangles by walking the faces one pair at a time in pure-Python networkx. `app/meshfix.py` does the same with scipy's compiled search: 28 times faster on its own; a 162,000-triangle figure with 30 % of its faces flipped converts with *Quick* in 9.4 s instead of 26.1 s, same result. Face-for-face the same as trimesh in 264 of 280 test cases (every one-piece mesh, with holes, two equal bodies); in the other 16, made-up multi-piece meshes with broken winding, a small piece is turned the other way round, where trimesh's own choice depends on Python's set order.
  - **Shape guessing:** the loop that finds blended cylinders and spheres called numpy's convenience functions about a million times on tiny arrays; they are now written out as the same operations in the same order. Checked bit-for-bit on every call of four full conversions (about 900,000 calls) and on the 11-mesh benchmark: all 35 comparable results identical, about 8 % faster in all (560 → 515 s).
  - Found and left open: the vase test part's *Classic* result varies between runs (28 or 30 areas kept as facets), with the old code as well.
- **Only the newest 4 releases are kept:** after making a new release, the publish workflow deletes older release pages. Their git tags stay, so every old version can still be downloaded from the Tags page. `KEEP: 4` in `.github/workflows/publish.yml`.
- **Clean-up:** unused counters and an unused import removed from both converters, an unused drop-in helper removed, and the one-off `tools/height_next_to_convert.py` deleted (its change to the Image tab is in the page since 0.7.1). Every web route the server answers is used by the page.
- **Description:** README, project page and `PUBLISHING.md` carry the line "Possibly the most advanced and functional free (for non-commercial use) solid conversion software in the world". The repository's own *About* description is set by hand (GitHub does not let the workflow do it); the text to paste is in `PUBLISHING.md`.

## 0.6.0-wip — drop-in plug-ins, ImageToSketch 0.9.0 as the first one, backend clean-up
- **Drop-in plug-ins (new, experimental).** Add-ons in the `dropins/` folder plug into fixed hooks of the app and are updated on their own, without a new STL2STEP. Plugins tab → *Drop-in plug-ins*: drop a zip to install or update (an update must be newer; a downgrade has to be asked for), *Switch off / on*, *Remove*, *Roll back* (the last two replaced copies are kept), each drop-in's version, state, hooks and last error. *Restart now* (Linux, macOS) restarts the app at the same address once no job is running and the page reloads by itself; on Windows quit and start again. Conversions use a new drop-in from the next job anyway. Command line: `stl2step.py dropins [list | install ZIP [--downgrade] | on|off|remove|rollback ID | pack FOLDER [ZIP]]`.
- **Hooks, version 1** (`app/hooks.py`): the Image → STEP tracer; extra tabs on the page and their requests (`/api/x/<id>/…`); extra command-line commands; a call when a job finishes. A drop-in says which hook versions it was written for; one written for others, needing a newer Python or a package that is not installed is set aside with a message. One that fails to load or raises in a hook is reported and the app and other drop-ins carry on. A misspelt hook is an error, not silence.
- Built on **pluggy** (MIT, the hook library of pytest; new in `app/requirements.txt`, the start file adds it on the next start, needs the internet once). The loader `app/plugkit.py` knows nothing about STL2STEP, so other apps can reuse it. Zips are checked (no paths outside their folder, no links, 300 MB limit). **A drop-in is Python code with your rights: install only ones you trust** (`SECURITY.md`).

## 0.5.2-wip — every colour change in a picture, and a diagnostic code in the preview

- **`stl2step diag`** reads a code back: `diag S2S1-...` or `diag screenshot.png` (a screenshot, or a photo of the screen).
- The code (`app/diagcode.py`, format `S2S1`) follows the conventions of the ErrCode project: several records multiplexed into one string, Crockford base32, the same minute time stamp and 15-bit tag, only the characters of QR's alphanumeric mode. About 90 characters, a 33 x 33 QR; the same facts as JSON would need 93 x 93. It has a check part (a mistyped character is noticed) and skips records it does not know. It does **not** contain ErrCode's error dictionary (that project's table is not in this repository): a failed job gets one of ten broad error classes and a tag of the message.
- **What is in the code:** app version, time (UTC, to the minute), job tag, kind of job, Linux / Windows / macOS, the settings of an STL → STEP conversion and the result numbers. **Not in it:** the file's name, paths, the log, the error text. Nothing is sent anywhere. Jetwash, SCAD and Image jobs carry the header and result only; the panel says so.

- The worker now also reports a job's numbers as data (a `{"d": ...}` line); the *Result* / *Original* buttons of the preview show which one is on.

## 0.4.0-wip — easy things first (EXPERIMENTAL Ladder engine)
- **Ladder engine** (`--engine ladder`, *Ladder* in Settings; off by default, Classic unchanged): the mesh is looked at for a few milliseconds (dihedral-angle histogram, exact-normal clusters with sharp borders, lathe test about candidate axes) and routed as **prismatic**, **lathe**, **organic** or **mixed**. Prismatic: Classic fitter with flat borders judged by edge length (keeps big-fillet and coarse-cylinder facets from passing as flats), never the slower Staged roughing. Lathe: surfaces of revolution are claimed on the whole mesh *before* the flat-face search (the plane search is what cost 8 of 12 s on the dense vase), then Staged roughing. Organic: the flat-face and cylinder/sphere searches are skipped and Guesstimate is switched on. Mixed: Classic with the caps and Guesstimate. `--route` forces a route; `--no-ladder-guess` keeps everything exact.
- **Caps** (any engine, via the Ladder options): `--budget S` (when passed, patches still outside tolerance become facets at once instead of being refined), `--max-fills N` (most slow plate fills), a cap on settling passes, and `--plane-rule length`.
- Measured on this 2-core Linux machine (Classic → Ladder): dense lathe vase 264 s / 8,980 faces → 8.0 s / 14 faces (0.025 mm); organic blob 17 s → 2.4 s (same 20 faces, 0.06 → 0.11 mm by Guesstimate); knob 17 s / 1,103 faces → 12 s / 951 faces (same 0.129 mm); fine stepped shaft 23 s / 2,190 faces (all facets) → 1.8 s / 8 faces (0.007 mm); big-fillet block 20 planes → 9 faces with the fillet as a true cylinder; bracket, plate and tray unchanged. **Tested on 13 synthetic CAD meshes and two organic blobs only; never on a scan or on the real target laptops.**
- **Potato-safe, with a time ceiling** (all engines): a performance profile now carries a *time ceiling* and a *plate-fill allowance* — Potato 2.5 h / 20 fills, Moderate and Extreme 40 min / 60–120 fills — after which anything still outside tolerance is kept as exact facets, so a run always finishes with a file (`--budget` / `--max-fills` override). A **checkpoint STEP** (exact faces as found, freeform areas as facets) is written before the slow freeform fitting on meshes of 20,000+ triangles (`checkpoint="on"` forces it on smaller ones), and if the fitter dies afterwards — out of memory included — that checkpoint is kept and reported instead of nothing. The plate filler is skipped when the process already holds three quarters of the computer's memory. The exact-faceted fallback reduces meshes of more than 20,000 triangles within half the tolerance first, so the file stays readable (a 60,000-face STEP takes minutes to open in some programs).
- **Checked against the heaviest test model and corrected** (160,000-triangle organic figure, Potato profile, this 2-core machine): the Ladder engine's *mixed* route was worse than Staged + Guesstimate (411 s, 14,217 faces, 0.44 mm against 268 s, 3,271 faces, 0.26 mm). The mixed route now claims lathe shapes first when at least half the curved area turns about one axis, and the Ladder's sharp-edge and flat-strip tweaks apply only to the prismatic and lathe routes; the same model now gives 3,271 faces at 0.26 mm in 318 s (25 s of that is the checkpoint), peak memory 1.3 GB. The checkpoint also failed to write on that model (the exact faces with facets between them did not close); it now falls back to a plain faceted solid, and is written with the Staged engine too.
- The time ceiling and plate-fill allowance now apply however a conversion is started (plain command line, the CAD plug-ins, the benchmark), using what the *auto* profile would pick for the computer; `--no-ceiling` switches both off. The lookup reads memory and core count only (a first version probed for a graphics card, which loaded PyTorch where the GPU pack is installed: +1.5 s and +450 MB per conversion).
- **Faster rebuilds** (all engines, identical output): when one face fails the geometry check and the solid is assembled again, faces whose triangles and boundary chains did not change are reused as deep copies instead of being rebuilt and re-checked (the knob skips about half its regions per repeat cycle: whole-shape checks 1,874 → 1,548).
- **Ladder engine:** flat regions are now measured by the smallest rectangle around their boundary, and a narrow strip is only rejected as a facet row when it is also within tolerance of the curved surface its soft borders turn through (sagitta rule). A subdivided knob that Classic turns into a 2,234-facet fallback in 86 s now converts in 20 s to 967 faces at 0.07 mm. The rule stays off in Classic because on that same mesh Classic then produced one 0.6 mm gap. The log also prints a rough time estimate for the part. Small closed results with no facets skip the STEP read-back.
- Research notes: `docs/research/SLICER.md` (slicer techniques: what transfers and what does not, with a measured prototype; nothing from it is built yet).
- Research notes: `docs/research/POTATO.md` (checkpointing, memory behaviour on 8 GB machines, what OpenCASCADE can and cannot interrupt, CPU ratios of the target laptops, and the measured face-cache and strip-width prototypes).
- Deviation check: a STEP face the mesher gives up on at the fine deflection is re-meshed coarser instead of being left as a hole (which was reported as a 0.5 mm deviation that wasn't there).
- Research notes: three more reports under `docs/research/` (cheap machine vision; combining the techniques; easy things first), summarised in `docs/research/README.md`.

## 0.3.0-wip — one app, three converters

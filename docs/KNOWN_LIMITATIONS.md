# Known limitations
- **Work in progress.** Expect rough edges.
- **First real model** (0.10.2 notes, [GHOST_CAT.md](../research/GHOST_CAT.md)): a 91,676-triangle organic cat on an M5 MacBook converted in 79 s (Draft) to 4 min 23 s (the old Standard, Classic), every result a valid closed solid; the old Standard's worst point was 1.110 mm from the mesh, outside its tolerance. Two defects that put Classic results millimetres off on two test plates were found and fixed in 0.10.2 ([STANDARD.md](../research/STANDARD.md)); whether either was the cat's is not known (the cat was not on the test machine). Most of the faces in organic results are facets around small sharp details (eyes, tatters); Quad patches refuses parts with many sharp edges, so on such a part Standard gives Quick's result.
- Organic shapes with many sharp rims or tunnels convert slowly and come out partly faceted (the black patches in the preview).
- *Staged*, *v2 fitter*, *Second pass*, *Extrude outline* and *Loft cross-sections* are experimental. *Staged* was tested only on a handful of synthetic parts and one organic model, on a 2-core Linux machine; on dense organic meshes at strict tolerance, or on non-watertight dense meshes, it can be much slower than Classic (see the Staged section above).
- The FreeCAD workbench and Fusion add-in, the one-click installer and the WebGL preview were tested only against stand-in mocks and headless Chromium, **not yet in real FreeCAD or Fusion**.
- The profiles, queue, scheduling and parallel Second pass were tested on a 2-core Linux machine only. The GPU pack path was verified to give identical results on the CPU build of PyTorch, but **not on a real Apple/Nvidia GPU**, and its speed-up is unmeasured. Card detection and the install choice (0.11.1) were tested against simulated tool output only; the install commands were never run against PyTorch's download server. The GTX 10-series route (CUDA 12.6 build) ends when PyTorch 2.14 is no longer installable. AMD and Intel graphics are never used. See [guide/gpu.md](guide/gpu.md).
- *Hardpoints* and *SCAD → STEP* were added in 0.3.0-wip and tested on this Linux machine only: four small hardpoint sample files, and OpenSCAD 2021.01 with one example model plus a `.csg` test. Many OpenSCAD features are covered but not every model has been tried; unsupported pieces fall back to flat facets from OpenSCAD's own mesh. The FreeCAD / Fusion *OpenSCAD* and *Export hardpoint STL* commands, and the right-click entries, were tested against stand-ins or fake home folders, **not in real FreeCAD, Fusion, Nemo, Nautilus or Windows**.
- *Image → STEP* was added in 0.5.0-wip and tested on this Linux machine only, on pictures drawn by the test code and on photographs from a simulated camera: **no printed token, no real phone photo, no real EXIF data from a phone**. Its STEP files were read back with OpenCascade and shown in the app's own preview; they were **not opened in FreeCAD, Fusion or any other CAD program**. Lens distortion is not corrected. The FreeCAD and Fusion plug-ins have no Image → STEP command.
- The *calibration sheet* (Image → STEP, 0.9.2-wip) was tried only on pictures drawn by the test code and on ImageToSketch's simulated photos: **no real print, no real stand, no real photo**, and not on Windows or macOS.
- *Every colour change* (Image → STEP, 0.5.2-wip) is experimental and was tried **only on pictures drawn by the test code**: no real icon or logo file, no real photograph. Drawn outlines between areas, the same colour on both sides of a thin line, very faint edges, and small or blurred pictures are known limits ([notes](../research/TRANSITION_TRACING.md)).
- *Quality presets* (0.9.0-wip) were measured on 12 test meshes and two big ones on Linux only. Draft's one-minute cap only cuts patch refining short. On a 220,000-triangle flat-sided tray Draft used not to finish at all (a bug in the flat-face reduction, fixed in 0.10.0: now 571 s, 25,759 faces, within tolerance; *Exact facets* does the same part in 428 s with 2,759 faces, and *Quick*, which does not reduce, did not finish it in 40 min). [Notes](../research/PRESETS.md).
- The *Quad patches* engine (0.10.1) was tested on five synthetic organic shapes on Linux only: no real scan or sculpt, QuadriFlow's build tool not run on Windows or macOS, Instant Meshes only on Linux. It uses more faces than Classic on busy shapes and can be slower (the bumpy ring 124 s); a very spiky blob needed 6,746 quads and 1.4 GB. [Notes](../research/QUADPATCHES.md).
- The winding repair added in 0.9.1 can turn a small separate piece of a badly wound multi-piece mesh the other way round from what trimesh did; neither is more right. (The vase's run-to-run difference noted in 0.9.1 was found and fixed in 0.10.0: results are repeatable again.)
- The *Diagnostic* panel (0.5.2-wip) was checked in headless Chromium on Linux: screenshots of the window read back; a photograph of a screen was only simulated. It carries the settings of STL → STEP conversions only, and no ErrCode error codes.
- The result is a solid of surfaces, not an editable feature history (no sketches / extrudes). See [HOW_IT_WORKS.md](../../HOW_IT_WORKS.md).
- Windows and macOS launchers were not run on real Windows/macOS by the author yet; Linux is the tested platform.

Picture

Turns a photo, scan or drawing of a flat part into its outline as real CAD curves (lines, arcs, circles, ellipses and splines; holes are found by nesting) and writes it as STEP: a solid of the thickness you choose, or the flat shape. A dark part on plain white paper, lit evenly, photographed from straight above works best. Engine: ImageToSketch.

Scale

The calibration sheet: print both pages at 100 %. Page 1 is a frame of numbered markers round a clear middle: it gives scale, tilt and the lens's bend, and warns when the paper is not flat. Print the stand (top face down), glue a label from page 2 on its top and measure its height with calipers; stood anywhere inside the frame, a few cm from the part, it measures the camera distance, so only the part's height needs typing (next to Convert to STEP). Measure page 2's two lines once if your printer may stretch the page.

The token is a black square with one clipped and one rounded corner. On paper: print the token sheet at 100 %, measure a square with a ruler, lay the part on the sheet clear of both squares; one square gives the scale, both in view measure the camera's tilt across the whole sheet. In plastic: print it once in matt black and lay it on white paper beside the part. With the camera distance known (a phone photo carries it) the part's height is taken out of the scale too: type it next to Convert to STEP.

Print the marker sheet at 100 % and lay the part in the middle with all four markers in view: it gives scale and corrects perspective in the plane of the paper.

STEP

  
  
  
  
 

 
Thickness 0 gives a flat face you can extrude in your CAD program; any other value gives a solid standing on Z = 0. Outlines inside outlines become holes; borderline (amber) shapes are left out unless you tick the box. A shape inside a cut-out (an island) is written as its own piece.
